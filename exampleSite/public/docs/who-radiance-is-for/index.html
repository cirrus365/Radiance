Who it is for

Radiance suits people publishing for themselves:

  • Personal homepages — a place that says who you are, what you are making and how to reach you.
  • Blogs — ordinary posts in ordinary windows, with an archive, tags and search that need no configuration beyond a content file.
  • Photography and sketch journals — images framed and processed by the theme, in moodboards or in plain post bodies.
  • Collections — moodboards of links, notes, palettes and sounds for anything you gather.

The desktop chrome is the point: it is nostalgic, small, quick and a little playful, and the site remains an ordinary website — links, scrolling, bookmarks.

Who it isn’t for

Being honest about the fit:

  • Business sites — services, pricing, teams, testimonials. The metaphor gets in the way of credibility rather than adding charm.
  • Shops — catalogues, carts and checkout flows need patterns this theme deliberately does not have.
  • Documentation portals — a handbook this size is the ceiling; deep, searchable technical documentation wants a docs-platform layout.
  • News layouts — dense front pages, live updates and headlines do not fit windows and desktops.

If your site is for an audience that expects your brand to look expensive and current, this theme is the wrong tool. If it is for people who will smile at a 28-pixel panel and keep reading, it is the right one.
